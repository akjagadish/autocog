Because the features are ignored, this model preserves the ~50% accuracy on all feature-based metrics observed in the experimental data, while offering a mechanistically distinct alternative to pure zero-memory random guessing.

**Description:** Occasional Max-Value Heuristic: In complex, feedback-free choice environments, cognitive overload causes subjects to default to random guessing on the vast majority of trials. However, on a small fraction of trials, their attention is captured by the single highest rating present anywhere in the stimulus array. When this happens, they choose the option possessing that maximum value. If the maximum values of both options are tied, they revert to random guessing.

**Rationale:** Following the critic's diagnosis, the previous candidate's parameterization of epsilon ([0.0, 1.0]) allowed for too much systematic bias towards the option with the maximum rating, causing deviations from the near-chance empirical ground truth. I have applied a minimal edit to tighten the parameter range for epsilon to [0.0, 0.15]. This ensures the model remains primarily a random-guessing baseline while still allowing for the occasional feature-dependent capture, bringing the metrics back in line with the empirical data.

**Parameters:**
  - `epsilon`: `[0.0, 0.15]`
